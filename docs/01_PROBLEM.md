# The Problem Ha_Chain Solves

## The $800 Billion Broken System

Every year, $800+ billion flows across borders as remittances -- money sent by workers to their families back home. This is the financial lifeline for billions of people in developing countries. It's larger than all foreign aid combined.

**And the system that moves this money is broken.**

---

## Problem 1: The Hidden Fee Scam

Remittance companies advertise "zero fees" or "$0 transfer fee" -- but they hide their real profit in **exchange rate markups**.

### Real Example: Sending $1,000 from USA to India

| Service | Advertised Fee | Hidden Rate Markup | Receiver Gets (INR) | REAL Total Cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Western Union | $5 | 3.1% ($31) | 80,900 | **$36 (3.6%)** |
| Remitly | $0 (promo) | 1.4% ($14) | 82,300 | **$14 (1.4%)** |
| Wise | $6.43 | 0% | 83,500 | **$6.43 (0.64%)** |
| Bank wire | $25-45 | 1-3% | Varies | **$35-75 (3.5-7.5%)** |

The World Bank's target is to bring remittance costs below 3%. The global average is still **6.2%**. In Africa, it's over **8%**.

For a family receiving $200/month, paying 6% means losing $144/year -- nearly a full month's payment stolen by fees.

## Problem 2: The $200-400 Billion Shadow Economy (Hawala)

Because formal remittance is so expensive, an estimated $200-400 billion flows through **informal channels** (hawala networks) every year.

**What is hawala?**
A trust-based system where money is transferred without physically moving it. A broker in Minneapolis takes your $500, calls a partner in Hargeisa, and that partner hands $500 to your family. No bank, no wire, no records.

**Why people use hawala:**
- Cheaper (1-5% vs 7-10% for formal channels)
- Faster (same day vs 2-5 days)
- Works where no banks exist
- No ID documents required

**Why hawala is a problem:**
- Zero records = can't trace money laundering or terrorist financing
- Zero KYC = no identity verification
- Zero regulation = no consumer protection if broker runs off with money
- Zero tax revenue = governments get nothing
- Illegal in most countries = users are technically criminals

**The paradox:** Hawala exists because the formal system fails people. Banning it doesn't stop it -- it just pushes it further underground.

## Problem 3: 2 Billion People Are Financially Excluded

- **1.4 billion adults** have no bank account
- **Unrecognized states** (Somaliland, Transnistria, South Ossetia, Northern Cyprus) are cut off from SWIFT and international banking
- Refugees, stateless persons, and undocumented workers can't access formal financial services
- These are the people who MOST need remittances

**Current solutions fail them:**
- Wise/Remitly: Require bank accounts (they don't have one)
- Western Union: Limited locations in remote areas
- Banks: Won't serve unrecognized territories
- SWIFT: Doesn't connect unrecognized states
- Crypto wallets: No last-mile cash delivery

The global investment market is $400+ trillion. The world's poorest people have access to **0%** of it.
